Who May Be Responsible When Medical Treatment in Türkiye Goes Wrong?

Learn how to identify the surgeon, hospital, clinic company and intermediary involved in treatment in Türkiye.

Start by identifying each participant

A clinic brand does not always identify the company that contracted with you or the facility where treatment took place. A claim may involve different people or organisations, and responsibility must be assessed separately for each one. A disappointing result alone does not establish fault.

Surgeon, hospital and contracting company

Record the treating clinician’s name and specialty, the actual hospital or clinic location, and the company named on consent forms, invoices and agreements. Compare these details with operating notes and discharge papers. The provider’s role, obligations and the evidence determine what legal route may be available.

Intermediary or booking agent

An intermediary may have arranged travel, handled payment or described the treatment. Save the booking agreement, advertisements, payment instructions and conversations identifying who made each statement. Its possible responsibility depends on its actual role and legal obligations; an introduction alone does not establish liability.

Build a provider timeline

List each organisation, its trading and legal names, address, contact details, service, date and payment. Retain original invoices, card statements, consent and treatment records. If a company has closed or changed its name, do not assume that an online brand identifies the correct legal entity. A lawyer can check the applicable records and evidence.

What an initial review can establish

A review can identify missing records and possible legal routes, but it cannot determine a provider’s responsibility without examining the treatment, promises, care, causation and loss. See our guide to a closed or renamed clinic and requesting records.
