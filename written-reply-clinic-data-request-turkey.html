A Turkish Clinic Answered Your Data Request by Phone: What Next?

Learn what KVKK’s October 2026 notice means for a clinic data request, how to obtain a written reply and how to track the complaint timetable.

Published 24 November 2026, 08:00 Türkiye time · Medical Law Türkiye

The answer you need after a telephone call

You submitted a personal data application to a Turkish clinic. Someone telephones, explains that the records department has considered it, and says the matter is closed. A useful conversation may have taken place, but you still need a response that you can read, retain and compare with your original questions. Start by asking for the final answer in writing or electronically, identifying the application and the date it reached the organisation. Keep the call details alongside the original submission rather than replacing that submission with a fresh, unrelated request.

On 1 October 2026, Türkiye’s Personal Data Protection Authority, KVKK, published a notice addressing precisely this problem. It explains that telephone or face-to-face contact can help clarify an application, but does not constitute the required final response. This is a clarification of existing duties under Article 13 of Law No. 6698 and Article 6 of the application communiqué; it does not introduce a new thirty-day period. The distinction matters to patients seeking an explanation of how a clinic uses their information, an amendment to inaccurate data or action concerning a disclosure. KVKK’s 1 October 2026 notice

Identify which request the clinic was answering

Find the exact message, letter or portal submission that prompted the call. Read it as an administrator seeing the file for the first time. Does it identify you, explain which personal data issue requires an answer and state what action you seek? A conversation about a refund, an appointment or a medical complication may contain private information without being a formal application exercising data protection rights. Give the document an accurate description. Calling every complaint a data request can make it harder to understand which procedure applies.

For example, a patient might have asked whether postoperative photographs were shared with a booking agency and requested the identity of recipients. Another might have challenged the wrong operation date in a patient file. A third might simply have asked the surgeon whether swelling was expected. Those questions require different responses and expertise. Separate the data issue from clinical questions in a short cover note. If an entry needs correcting, the steps in our medical-record correction guide help define the disputed information before discussing the reply.

Locate the organisation responsible for the data

Check the clinic’s privacy notice, invoice, admission paperwork and legal company name. The surgeon’s assistant, hospital patient-relations team and travel coordinator may communicate with you through the same messaging group while working for different organisations. Ask which entity received your application and which entity will provide its final answer. Record the sender’s role when a call comes from an unfamiliar number. This small administrative check can prevent a prolonged exchange with somebody who cannot answer on the record holder’s behalf.

Suppose a coordinator says that a hospital has approved deletion of an advertising photograph. Ask whether the coordinator is relaying the hospital’s decision, answering for the agency’s own account, or promising to contact another party. Keep each account or publication destination identifiable. An assurance concerning one website does not tell you what happened on a separate social-media profile. Our guide to identifying treatment providers can help organise the entities, although determining responsibility for particular data processing requires its own assessment.

Check the original application’s language and route

The application communiqué provides for applications in Turkish and specifies recognised submission methods. These include a written application, registered electronic mail, secure electronic or mobile signature, an email address previously notified to and recorded by the controller, or an application system designed for that purpose. It also lists identifying and contact information, including nationality and a passport or available identity number for foreign applicants. The date of delivery or receipt matters. Check the actual submission against these requirements rather than assuming that any informal chat started the statutory timetable. Application communiqué, Articles 4–5

If you initially wrote only in English to a sales number, preserve that exchange and obtain help putting the formal application into the appropriate form. Do not alter the original message to make it look compliant retrospectively. Keep a separate Turkish version and identify the earlier correspondence accurately. A translation should preserve the distinction between requesting information, disputing accuracy and seeking deletion. Before transmitting identification, confirm the recipient through the clinic’s established channels and ask how verification should be completed securely. An open group chat is a poor place to circulate a full passport scan.

Make a factual note while the conversation is fresh

Write down the date, time, time zone, incoming number, caller’s stated name and role, and the application reference mentioned. Summarise what was said without turning an uncertain recollection into a quotation. Distinguish a definite refusal from a promise to investigate, an identity-check question or an explanation of where records are held. Keep any call log already available on your device. You do not need to start secretly recording calls to create a useful chronology; recording and disclosure raise separate legal issues that require individual advice.

A practical note might say that a patient-relations officer called at 14:10 Turkish time, confirmed receipt of a named application and stated that a reply would be emailed after checking the archive. That describes progress, not a final decision. If the officer instead said that the hospital would not identify any recipients, record that statement and request the organisation’s written position and reasons. Avoid adding conclusions such as “admitted malpractice” or “proved concealment” when the conversation concerned only administrative handling of a data request.

Send a focused follow-up linked to the existing file

Use the established application channel and keep the subject line stable. Identify the original delivery date, reference number and brief purpose. Thank the staff member for explaining the process, then ask for the final answer in a durable form. Restate the unanswered points as short, separately numbered questions in the actual application. The follow-up should let a reader locate the existing file immediately. It need not contain your entire treatment history or attach every photograph and invoice again.

An English drafting outline for translation is: “I refer to my personal data application delivered on the stated date and your telephone call on the stated date. Please provide the organisation’s final response in writing or electronically, identifying which requests are accepted, which are refused and the reasons for refusal. Please also identify any action already completed and the documents supplied.” Adapt the wording to the facts and obtain an accurate Turkish version for formal use. This outline is a communication aid; it does not establish that an earlier submission was valid or extend a complaint deadline.

Read the substance of any written response

When the answer arrives, compare it point by point with the request. A delivery acknowledgement confirms that something entered the system; it does not explain whether the organisation accepted your demands. A generic privacy policy describes general practices but may leave your specific questions unanswered. Look for an identifiable controller or representative, the application under consideration, the organisation’s explanation and what action it has taken. The communiqué distinguishes acceptance from a reasoned refusal and requires notification of the response in writing or electronically. Application communiqué, Article 6

Create a simple comparison in your working notes: requested information, answer received, supporting attachment and remaining question. If the clinic says an incorrect date has been amended, locate the replacement record and check the exact field. If it says it has ceased a particular disclosure, ask which activity that description covers. Do not infer that every stored copy has been destroyed. Accurate clinical records may need to be retained under applicable rules even when an unrelated promotional use is disputed. Keep questions about lawful retention separate from questions about public availability.

Open the attachments and verify that you can use them

A response email may contain a password-protected archive, expiring link or portal instruction. Check promptly whether the files open, whether the password arrived through the promised channel and whether the material concerns you. Note any unreadable page, empty folder or missing attachment by filename. Ask for a usable replacement without silently editing the supplied original. Save a copy of the message and the attachments together, with their receipt dates, so that the relationship between the explanation and the documents remains visible.

For a concrete example, imagine that the answer lists an operation report and a consent form but the download contains only a billing statement. State that discrepancy precisely. “The attachment named consent contains an invoice” gives the records officer a checkable problem. “Nothing is correct” does not. If a file appears to concern another person, stop circulating it, tell the verified organisation through a private channel and ask for secure instructions. Our medical-record request guide explains how to describe the documents needed for a clinical or legal review.

Keep the complaint timetable separate from reminders

Law No. 6698 generally requires the controller to conclude the application as soon as appropriate and within thirty days. For a rejected, insufficiently answered or unanswered application, Article 14 provides a complaint route after the controller application stage. It states a period of thirty days from learning the response and, in every case, sixty days from the application. These are linked rules, not interchangeable waiting periods. Record the relevant dates and obtain a case-specific calculation if you may complain. Do not assume that another reminder, telephone call or promised investigation restarts the clock. KVKK’s procedural guidance quoting Articles 13–14

Keep a dated chronology with separate entries for dispatch, confirmed receipt, oral contact, written response and the date you actually learned that response. Preserve evidence supporting each entry. If the original delivery date is disputed, mark it as disputed rather than choosing the most convenient one. Seek advice promptly when a deadline may be approaching. The October notice helps explain why an oral conversation is not the required final answer, but it is not a reason to leave your file untouched until somebody chooses to call again.

Handle partial answers without losing the original question

Sometimes the clinic supplies part of the requested information and says the remainder belongs to another department. Acknowledge what has arrived, identify the outstanding items and ask for the organisation’s position on those items. Keep the complete original request attached to the working file. If you narrow a later question to help locate a document, make clear which earlier issue remains unresolved. This prevents a series of helpful but fragmented exchanges from obscuring the fact that one central point has never been addressed.

Consider a patient asking about access to postoperative images. The hospital provides a list of internal departments but says nothing about whether an external booking company received copies. The patient can note that the internal list is received while separately identifying the unanswered external-disclosure question. That preserves the distinction between dissatisfaction with an answer and absence of an answer to a particular point. A lawyer reviewing the correspondence can then assess the actual scope of the response without reconstructing it from scattered screenshots.

Arrange language support and representation carefully

If you cannot discuss detailed records comfortably in Turkish, ask whether language support is available and request time to read the written response. Tell the interpreter which terms concern data handling and which concern medical care. Keep the Turkish original beside any translation. If the translation uses a stronger expression than the original, clarify it before relying on it in a complaint. A sentence saying that records are being reviewed should not become a statement that a breach has been accepted.

When a relative assists, identify whether they are helping you organise correspondence or acting as an authorised representative. Ask the organisation what evidence of authority is required for the requested disclosure. Being copied into a family email does not by itself resolve access questions. Share a compact file with your adviser: the application, receipt evidence, call note, final reply and a short list of unresolved issues. There is usually no benefit in sending an adviser an unlabelled export of every personal conversation when a clear index can explain what matters.

Continue clinical care through the appropriate channel

A data application is an administrative and legal process. It should not become the only route for asking about symptoms, medication or an approaching follow-up appointment. Send clinical concerns to the treating team or a suitable local clinician, clearly identifying their purpose. If care is urgent, seek appropriate medical help promptly. Keep the medical question and the records question in separate messages so that a staff member does not mistake a request for clinical assistance for another reminder about paperwork.

For instance, a patient may need an operative note for a specialist appointment while a broader data complaint remains unresolved. Explain the appointment date and ask whether the needed document can be supplied through the records service. Preserve any refusal or alternative offered, but do not postpone a necessary consultation solely to complete the complaint file. Your clinician can advise what information is essential now. An incomplete administrative response and an adverse treatment outcome are separate matters; neither automatically establishes the other.

Prepare a compact file if the issue remains unresolved

Before seeking legal advice or preparing a complaint, put the material in chronological order. Use filenames that describe the document and its date. Keep originals unchanged, and put annotations in a separate note. Include the legal company details you have verified, the application route used, proof of delivery, any identity-check exchange, the call chronology and the written answers. State the remedy or information still sought in a few factual sentences. The resulting file should be understandable without access to your phone.

A useful closing check is whether an independent reader can answer four questions: what you asked, who received it, what they answered and what remains missing. If one answer is uncertain, label the uncertainty and identify the document that might resolve it. The aim is a reliable account of the process, not a dramatic narrative.
