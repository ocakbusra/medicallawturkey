The Clinic Offered a Free Revision or Refund—What Should I Check Before Signing a Release?

Questions to ask before accepting a revision or refund after cosmetic surgery in Türkiye, including the document’s scope, parties and payment terms. Estimated reading time: 6 minutes

Short answer

A free revision or refund offer does not, by itself, explain what a proposed release or settlement would cover. Before signing, obtain the complete document, understand every clause and party it names, and get independent legal advice on how it may affect your specific situation.

Updated: 25 September 2026. This is general legal information, not advice on an individual matter.

Separate the offer from the document

A clinic may offer a corrective procedure, a partial refund, reimbursement of travel expenses or another form of assistance after a patient raises a concern. Those practical options are separate from the written document the clinic asks you to sign. The document may describe the offer, set conditions, record a settlement or include a release of claims. Its effect depends on its wording, the parties and the applicable law.

Do not assume that accepting treatment automatically ends a legal claim, or that a refund is an admission of fault. Equally, do not assume that a form called a “receipt,” “consent” or “administrative document” has no legal effect. Read the full text before agreeing.

If you need urgent medical care, prioritise care from an appropriately qualified clinician. You can ask for time to review the document without delaying necessary treatment. A legal review is not a substitute for a medical decision.

Seven points to check in a proposed release

  1. Who is making the offer? Confirm the full legal names of the clinic, hospital, surgeon, intermediary or other party named—not only a brand or social-media account.
  2. What exactly is being offered? Identify the procedure, amount, timing, travel or accommodation terms, and who pays each item.
  3. What does the document say you give up? Look for language about past, present or future claims, unknown losses, treatment costs or claims against other named parties.
  4. What happens if the revision is delayed or does not proceed? Check the conditions, deadlines, cancellation terms and what happens if a clinician advises against the proposed procedure.
  5. Are there confidentiality or non-disparagement clauses? Understand what communications are restricted, how long the restriction applies and whether exceptions are stated.
  6. Is the document bilingual? Compare both versions. Do not sign a translation you cannot understand or assume that the English text controls.
  7. How and when is the agreement completed? Check signatures, authority to sign, payment method, receipts and any conditions that must be met first.

This is a review checklist, not a statement that each clause is enforceable or unenforceable. A lawyer should assess the entire document against the facts before you sign.

Questions to ask before deciding

Ask for the full document in advance and a copy for your records. Useful written questions include:

  • Which legal entity is making the offer, and which parties would be covered by the release?
  • Does the offer depend on signing a waiver, confidentiality term or admission?
  • Does the offer cover only the listed payment or procedure, or does it address other losses too?
  • Can you obtain independent medical advice about the proposed revision before agreeing?
  • What happens to the offer if your condition changes or another clinician advises different care?
  • Can you have a qualified translator explain the document before a decision is required?

Ask for the answer in writing. Keep the offer, earlier complaints, clinical records and any draft agreement in their original form.

Preserve your position without escalating the dispute

Save the exact offer and the context in which it arrived: date, sender, email address or phone number, attachments and any stated deadline. Do not edit messages or post private documents publicly. If you reply, keep the message factual and avoid statements you do not intend to make.

Make a separate list of known losses and future questions, such as further clinical review, travel, translation or time away from work. Do not assume that a suggested figure covers every loss, or that every expense is legally recoverable. The Turkish Code of Obligations contains general rules on obligations and compensation, but applying them to a medical-treatment dispute is fact-specific.

Read our related guides on how compensation is assessed and medical malpractice and compensation liability. If there is a response deadline, ask a Turkish lawyer promptly whether it affects your options.

When an independent review is especially useful

Consider getting legal advice before signing if the document refers to “full and final settlement,” releases multiple parties, is only available in Turkish, asks you to waive future claims, or makes the revision conditional on a broad promise. These phrases are signals to read carefully, not automatic proof of a particular legal result.

If you are being asked to decide during a short clinic visit or while travelling, request a copy and a reasonable opportunity to review it. An initial legal review can identify the parties, clauses and questions that need attention. It cannot guarantee a negotiation result or replace a clinical opinion about whether a revision is appropriate.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a free revision automatically end my right to bring a claim?

Not necessarily. The effect depends on what you agree to and the surrounding facts. Review any written conditions or release before signing.

Does accepting a refund mean the clinic admits fault?

A payment offer alone does not establish what the clinic admits. Check the wording of the offer and agreement.

Can I ask for time to review a release?

You can ask for the complete document and time to understand it. If a medical issue is urgent, seek clinical care while arranging legal review.

Should I sign if the document is only in Turkish?

Do not rely on a document you cannot understand. Obtain a reliable translation and independent legal advice before signing.
