Dental Treatment Gone Wrong in Türkiye: Records for a Legal Review

What international patients can preserve after problematic dental implants, crowns or veneers in Türkiye.

Put your current health first

If you have pain, swelling, infection or another concern, seek assessment from a qualified dental professional promptly. Do not delay necessary care while waiting for a legal review. An unwanted outcome does not by itself prove negligent treatment.

Request the treatment file

Ask the provider for the treatment plan, consent documents, clinical notes, imaging, laboratory information, invoices, discharge or aftercare instructions and records of the actual treatment. Note the clinician, facility and dates. The records request guide explains how to organise a request.

Keep a clear before-and-after chronology

Preserve pre-treatment scans and existing dental history where available, the dates of procedures and symptoms, messages to the provider and advice received after returning home. Keep originals and avoid changing file metadata where possible. Note uncertainty rather than filling gaps from memory.

Follow-up and corrective work

Ask your treating dentist to document findings, proposed treatment and reasons in their own clinical records. Keep estimates, invoices and proof of payment. Do not postpone clinically necessary treatment to preserve evidence; ask your clinician what records can be retained safely.

What a legal assessment considers

A review may consider the agreed treatment, information provided, standard of care, causation, loss and applicable legal route. Evidence of a problem or a corrective estimate does not automatically establish liability. See our dental malpractice page and home-country records guide.

Official sources

Check the current text and obtain advice on how it applies to your circumstances.

  • Patient Rights Regulation, Ministry of Health
  • Turkish Code of Obligations, Law No. 6098

Reconstruct the original plan

Collect the original examination findings, treatment proposal, price quote and any messages about the proposed teeth, materials and number of visits. If the plan changed, note who explained the change and when. Ask for consent forms and any records of discussions about alternatives, risks and expected maintenance. A promotional description is not necessarily the same as the treatment actually agreed. Keep both, and mark any point that is based on your recollection rather than a document.

Ask for complete clinical records

Depending on the treatment, useful records can include pre-treatment and post-treatment imaging, charting, clinical notes, laboratory orders, implant or material identification, prescriptions and follow-up instructions. Ask the provider which items exist and how copies can be supplied. Under Article 16 of the Patient Rights Regulation, patients may inspect and obtain a copy of records about their health directly or through an authorised representative. The procedure for a particular request should be checked with the provider.

Document follow-up at home

Tell your current dentist about the treatment and symptoms, including any pre-existing conditions. Ask that findings, tests and treatment recommendations be recorded in the normal clinical file. If further work is recommended, keep the proposed plan, itemised estimate and later invoices. A clinician may not be able to determine the cause from a single appointment; do not ask them to state a legal conclusion outside their assessment. Necessary care should never be delayed while waiting for a legal file to be assembled.

Preserve communications and payments

Save full conversations about symptoms, aftercare, requests for records and any proposed revision. Include dates, the sender's identity where known and attachments, rather than preserving isolated screenshots alone. Keep receipts and identify any payments made to an intermediary as well as the dental facility. If a provider offers to correct the work, ask for the proposal and any release in writing. The decision about further clinical treatment should be made with appropriate professional advice, not under pressure to sign.

Questions for the initial review

Which clinician and facility provided each stage? Are the images and clinical notes complete? What did the follow-up dentist observe, and what is still uncertain? What expenses are already paid and what is only an estimate? A lawyer can use this information to identify missing evidence and potential routes, but neither an adverse result nor the need for a replacement automatically proves malpractice or a recoverable amount.

When records or imaging are missing

Keep a list of each document requested, the date of the request and the reply. If the clinic says an image or laboratory record is held elsewhere, ask for the name and contact details of the organisation that holds it. Existing photographs can help establish a chronology, but they should not be presented as a substitute for clinical imaging or an examination. Retain originals and copies without editing dates or annotations into the originals. A lawyer can advise how a further request or other step should be approached if a material item remains unavailable.
