What this guide covers

“French sling” or Fransız askısı commonly refers to a cosmetic facial thread lift in Turkey. If you experienced persistent pain, visible threads, asymmetry or an unexpected result, first establish exactly which technique and material were used.

A medical complication and legal responsibility are different questions. The assessment depends on the clinical history, information given before treatment, standard of care, causation and actual loss.

What procedure does “French sling” mean?

Clinics use this English phrase for Fransız askısı, a facial thread-lift treatment intended to lift areas of the face or neck. Memorial Hospital describes Fransız askısı as a thread lift. It is not a urethral sling operation. The exact threads, placement and promised duration can vary; the invoice, consent form and treatment notes should identify the procedure actually supplied.

  • Ask for the product name and material, treatment sites, practitioner identity and treatment record.
  • Preserve advertisements or messages about the expected effect and how long it would last.
  • Record any later removal, adjustment or corrective care.

Symptoms and the legal question

Thread lifts can involve irritation, infection, visible threads or an unsatisfactory result. A temporary effect or mild early swelling does not by itself show substandard care. A review focuses on patient selection, informed consent, technique, complication management and whether a claimed injury is supported by clinical records.

  • Persistent pain, unusual redness, fever or a thread breaking through the skin calls for medical assessment.
  • Dated images help show when asymmetry or skin changes appeared.
  • If an independent clinician recommends treatment, keep that written assessment and its reasons.

Why the wording of the offer matters

A thread lift and a surgical facelift are not equivalent. Compare what you were promised with the actual procedure and the limitations explained before you agreed. For related aesthetic procedures, see our non-surgical treatment guide.

Evidence specific to a facial thread lift

The materials and technique matter. Request the treatment chart showing the product and number of threads, insertion points, practitioner, anaesthetic and aftercare instructions. Keep the consent document and any marketing claim about a particular lifting effect or duration. Without those details, it can be difficult to distinguish an expected temporary result from a dispute about what was provided.

If another clinician examines a palpable or visible thread, ask them to describe the finding without guessing who was at fault. Record the recommendation, any removal or treatment, and the associated expenses. A lawyer can then assess the clinical evidence together with the provider’s explanations and agreement.

Patient rights and legal review in Turkey

The Turkish Patient Rights Regulation addresses information, consent and access to records. The exact claim route and deadlines depend on the provider relationship and the facts. An independent medical assessment may be needed before a lawyer can advise whether treatment or aftercare fell below the applicable standard. See the Patient Rights Regulation (Ministry of Justice).

If there is a viable claim, the value is not determined by the name of the procedure. Documented treatment expense, income impact and other proven effects are considered under the relevant legal rules. Read how compensation is assessed for a fuller explanation.
