Can I Request My Medical Records From a Turkish Clinic After Returning Home?

Learn how international patients can request Turkish medical records, what to include, and how to keep a clear record of the request. Estimated reading time: 7 minutes

Short answer

Yes. Article 16 of Türkiye’s Patient Rights Regulation says a patient may inspect health records and obtain a copy, directly or through an authorised representative or legal representative. Ask the facility in writing for a complete, legible copy and confirm its secure delivery process.

Updated: 25 September 2026. This is general legal information, not advice on an individual matter.

Why the records matter

After returning home, you may need to understand what happened during treatment, give a local clinician accurate information, or ask a Turkish lawyer to review a concern. A complete file helps those people work from the same chronology. It can also help distinguish what was planned, what was performed, what was recorded afterwards, and what follow-up was offered.

A record request is a practical first step, not a conclusion that malpractice occurred. A complication or an unsatisfactory result does not by itself establish legal fault. The medical records need to be considered alongside the treatment plan, consent discussion, clinical context and later care.

Article 16 of the Patient Rights Regulation provides that the patient may inspect records about their health and obtain a copy, either personally or through a duly authorised representative or legal representative. The regulation also covers public and private health institutions. The facility’s own identity-check and delivery procedure may still need to be followed.

What to ask the clinic or hospital for

Request the records connected with the relevant assessment, procedure and follow-up. Use a list so that a short summary or discharge sheet is not mistaken for the full file.

  • Consultation notes, medical history, examination findings and pre-operative assessment.
  • All consent forms, patient-information documents and versions supplied in your language.
  • The operation or procedure report, including the date, facility and names or roles recorded for the treating team.
  • Anaesthesia records, medication charts, nursing observations and recovery-room notes, where applicable.
  • Laboratory results, imaging, photographs and other test results that form part of your file.
  • Discharge instructions, prescriptions, aftercare notes and messages documenting follow-up or referrals.
  • Invoices, itemised payment records and the name of the legal entity that issued them.

Ask for a complete copy in a readable format and, if the facility offers it, an index of the documents supplied. If a category does not exist or is held by another provider, ask the facility to identify that clearly rather than assuming it was omitted.

A practical request template

Adapt this request to the facility’s official email or patient-record channel. Do not send passport details or sensitive health information to an address you have not verified. Ask the facility which secure identification and delivery method it accepts.

Subject: Request for a copy of my medical records

Dear Patient Records / Patient Rights Unit,

I am requesting a complete copy of the medical records relating to my care at [facility name] from [date] to [date]. The care concerned [brief procedure or visit]. Please include the consultation and assessment notes, consent and patient-information documents, procedure or operation report, anaesthesia and recovery records if applicable, test results, medication and nursing records, discharge instructions, and follow-up notes.

Please tell me your identity-verification requirements and the secure method available for receiving the records. If any requested record is held by another provider or is not available, please identify that category and the appropriate contact point.

Name: [full name]
Date of birth or patient number: [as requested through the verified channel]
Dates of treatment: [dates]
Preferred response language: [language]
Contact details: [secure contact details]

Kind regards,
[name]

If you need a Turkish version, ask a fluent translator or counsel to translate the request accurately. Keep the original and sent version together.

How to send and track the request

  1. Verify the provider name and official contact route on your invoice, discharge documents or the provider’s official website.
  2. Send a dated written request and save the exact message, attachments, delivery confirmation and any reply.
  3. Ask whether the records can be provided electronically and how the facility will verify your identity.
  4. If another person will collect the file, ask what signed authority or representation documents the provider requires.
  5. When a response arrives, save the original files without editing them. Make a separate working copy for translation or annotation.

Keep a simple log with the date, recipient, channel, response and any documents received. If you receive only a partial file, reply with a short list of the missing categories and ask whether they are held elsewhere. A lawyer can advise on the appropriate next step if the request remains unresolved.

What to do with the records once they arrive

Preserve the files as received, including email headers, download links and any accompanying index. Store a backup securely. Do not overwrite the originals while adding notes; create a separate chronology that points back to the source file and page number.

If the documents are in Turkish, first identify which items need translation for a clinical second opinion or a legal review. A court may require a Turkish translation of foreign-language documents submitted as evidence, and may ask for an official translation. That is a separate issue from preparing an informal working translation for an initial review.

For a case-specific assessment, a reviewer may need to compare the records with your own timeline, communications and any later treatment. See our guides to patient rights and informed consent and legal rights after cosmetic-surgery complications.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a foreign patient request records after leaving Türkiye?

The Patient Rights Regulation allows the patient to inspect and obtain a copy of health records. The facility may ask you to complete its identity-verification and delivery steps.

Can my lawyer or family member request the records for me?

The regulation refers to an attorney or legal representative. A family member should not assume that relationship alone is enough; ask the facility which authority document it requires.

What if the clinic sends only a discharge summary?

Reply in writing with the specific record categories you still need and ask whether the records are held by another facility or provider. Keep a copy of both requests.

Do I need a certified translation before asking for a legal review?

Not always for an initial review. A court submission can have different translation requirements, so confirm what is needed before paying to translate a large file.
