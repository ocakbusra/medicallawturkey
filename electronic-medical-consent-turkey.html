Electronic Medical Consent in Türkiye: What an SMS Code Confirms

Learn how Türkiye’s October 2026 electronic consent rules work, who enters the procedure code and how to retrieve the form after treatment.

Published 21 November 2026, 16:29 Türkiye time · Medical Law Türkiye

A code arrives while you are waiting for surgery

A text arrives on your phone at a clinic in Türkiye. The receptionist says the doctor is ready. The message contains six digits. Before reading them aloud, ask one question: what exactly would those digits confirm? Updating a telephone number, opening a hospital portal and agreeing to an operation are three different actions. The answer matters especially when the screen belongs to somebody else or the language on it is unfamiliar.

On 27 October 2026, Türkiye published a regulation governing how informed consent can be recorded electronically before a medical intervention. It entered into force on publication and covers primary, secondary and tertiary health facilities. The change concerns the electronic route when a facility uses it. A patient visiting for dental work, an outpatient procedure or elective surgery may encounter the new process. The practical difference is that there should be an identifiable form, a verified number, a professional responsible for the transaction and a record of the decision. Read the published text of the regulation

Find out what the first message does

Consider a patient who bought a local SIM at the airport. Her booking was made using a number from home. At registration, the clerk asks which number she intends to use. Before any code arrives, she can ask staff to read back the last few digits, check the country prefix and explain whether they are verifying her number or asking for a treatment decision. She can then keep the handset in her own possession and listen to the clinical explanation without juggling a succession of alerts.

Article 7 distinguishes verification through e-Nabız, the national personal health system, from verification through the facility's own system. Where the latter verifies a previously unverified number through the prescribed procedure, the facility records it as verified for twelve months. That period concerns the number. A patient's decision about a particular intervention is a separate event. Someone returning for a revision six months later can therefore ask for the proposed revision form even if the same mobile number is still on file. Ministry text, Articles 7 and 8

Read the form before deciding

Ask the clinician to identify the intervention in ordinary words. If the appointment is for a dental bridge, and the screen mentions an extraction, pause and find out whether removing a tooth is part of the proposal, a possible contingency or an error. A diagram, a tooth number and a description in the patient's language may resolve the question within minutes. A booking reference generally cannot resolve it. When the form changes, establish which version reflects the revised plan before deciding.

The same approach helps with a bilateral operation. Imagine that the consultation concerned both eyelids, but the document names one side and leaves a second procedure under a broad heading. Point to the exact wording and ask the person who will perform the intervention to explain the intended extent. Say what you believe is agreed and invite correction. A companion can take notes if you wish. Neither a note nor a translation of a heading supplies the missing clinical answer. How to arrange a medical interpreter

A calm way to open the discussion is to ask, in your own words, what will happen if you proceed, what alternatives are available and what postponement would mean in your circumstances. One patient may care most about returning to work; another may need to lift a child or manage stairs. Tell the professional which concern could change your decision. Ask for an explanation you can follow and enough time to consider it. Turkish patient-rights rules address the substance of this conversation alongside the newer electronic procedure. Patient Rights Regulation

Identify who enters the intervention code

Under Article 8, the professional makes the form available for review. Access can come through e-Nabız or a link sent to the verified mobile. If the patient accepts the intervention, a one-time code is generated for that intervention. In the e-Nabız route the patient enters it directly in the relevant part of e-Nabız. In the facility route the patient shares that intervention code with the health professional conducting the process, who submits it using the facility's electronic communication device. These steps explain why identifying the route comes before deciding whom to speak to. Electronic consent regulation, Article 8

Picture a coordinator contacting you through a social account the night before admission. The sender says that payment and consent must be completed together and asks for whatever code comes next. Finish that exchange without disclosing a password or code. Contact the hospital through the telephone number on your verified appointment documents. Ask its clinical team which process it uses, whose name you should recognise and when you will review the form. A travel agent arranging a transfer has a different job from the clinician recording your decision.

A similar misunderstanding can happen across a desk with no wrongdoing intended. A staff member may be trying to restore wireless access while a consent message lands at the same moment. Read the purpose shown in each notification. If only part of the message appears on the lock screen, open it privately. Ask for the explanation again if the device is passed between people. Avoid forwarding a screenshot of your inbox, which might expose unrelated account details along with a time-sensitive code.

If the phone or the language gets in the way

A traveller may have mobile data but no incoming texts. A roaming restriction, a lost handset or a mistyped prefix can look identical from the waiting room. Describe what you can actually observe. For instance, a booking email arrived yesterday, a verification message never appeared today and no consent form has been reviewed. Ask how staff can correct the recorded contact details and what documented alternative is available if electronic verification cannot be completed. Using a driver's telephone simply because it has better reception can make the record harder to interpret.

Accessibility deserves its own conversation. Someone with low vision may need magnification, a larger display or an opportunity to listen to the text privately. Someone with hearing loss may prefer written questions followed by a face-to-face explanation. A patient with limited dexterity may need the device placed on a stable surface. State the barrier and the adjustment that would help. An assistant can steady a phone or explain a menu without making a treatment decision. If the arrangements still leave you unable to understand an elective intervention, tell the clinician which point remains unresolved.

When a minor or a person under legal restriction needs a legal representative, Article 11 applies the identity and mobile verification requirements to the representative. If that representative is absent, unavailable or cannot be electronically verified as required, electronic consent cannot be obtained under that provision. A grandparent accompanying a teenager on holiday should therefore raise representation with the facility before travelling. Ask what documents the facility needs, who will check them and when the actual representative must be available. Other rules, including those for emergencies, require their own assessment. Electronic consent regulation, Article 11

If the plan changes after you reviewed it

A practical difficulty arises when a new finding changes an elective proposal. Suppose the booking concerned scar revision, but during consultation a larger procedure involving another area is discussed. Ask the clinician to name what has been added, explain its implications and show how the revised plan will be recorded. If you accept one component and want more time for another, say so explicitly. Ask staff to read back what they understood before they prepare equipment or record a final decision.

An international patient might also discover that a marketing brochure names one surgeon while the person explaining the form is someone else. Write down both names with their roles. The person arranging travel, the professional recording consent and the person performing treatment may be different. Ask how the team identifies each participant and who can answer questions about technique, recovery and alternatives. If a shift changes halfway through, explain which questions have already been answered so that the next conversation starts from the right place. Patient guide to a change of surgeon

Retrieve the record before travelling home

The regulation provides for the completed electronic form to be transferred to the Ministry's central health data system and made accessible to the patient through e-Nabız. Ask staff where you can find the document after completion. A visitor who cannot access the portal should explain that difficulty while still in contact with the hospital and ask its records office how to request an accessible copy. Record the department's official contact details, the date of attendance and the name used at registration. Keep a downloaded original separately from annotated copies. How to request medical records from Türkiye

If two relatives attended the same clinic on the same afternoon, sort their documents before either file leaves the room. Check the patient's name, facility, procedure and date. A family tablet can easily display the previous person's download. Close the session after saving your own file in a private location. If another person's information appears, report the mismatch privately to the facility and ask for a corrected delivery. Circulating the mistaken attachment in a group chat unnecessarily exposes its contents.

Reconstruct a disputed event without guessing

Imagine receiving a discharge summary that refers to consent at half past nine, although you remember speaking to the surgeon later. Begin with a short chronology. Separate the time a message arrived, the time the form appeared, the approximate consultation time and any later confirmation. Note whether the phone displayed local time or your home time zone. Mark estimates as estimates. A timestamp from a cropped image is difficult to interpret without the date, the surrounding exchange and an explanation of whose device produced it.

Article 9 requires retention of transaction information including the date and time of the electronic declaration, records concerning dispatch and verification of the intervention code and information about the professional involved. The record also includes safeguards concerning integrity and timestamps. Ask the facility to identify the particular transaction and preserve the associated material if a dispute exists. A patient copy and an institutional audit record serve different purposes. The professional reviewing the issue can advise which additional records may be obtainable through the proper channel. Electronic consent regulation, Article 9

Keep your description factual. For example, write that you saw a link while standing at reception, that you asked about the anaesthetic when seated with a nurse and that you do not recall seeing the amended page. Identify a witness only if that person was actually present. Preserve the document as received, put later recollections in a separately dated note and describe any translation assistance. If the clinic replies that the number was verified last spring, ask for the information relating to this intervention on this visit. It is a narrower question and usually easier for a records officer to locate.

A short request you can adapt

When contacting a hospital from abroad, a useful opening would be: I attended your outpatient department on the afternoon shown on my appointment confirmation. Please identify the consent form associated with my procedure and advise how I can obtain the completed version securely. I used a temporary Turkish number during the visit and now use my usual number. Please tell me how you verify identity for the request before I send personal documentation. Add the precise question you need answered, such as whether an attachment displayed during consultation formed part of the approved version.

You can adapt the same request for an appointment that has yet to happen. Explain which language you read comfortably, whether you can receive texts overseas, and any practical difficulty using a touchscreen. Arrange a named contact at the facility and allow a little time between registration and the clinical discussion. When the phone eventually lights up, you will know who sent the message, which step it belongs to and whether you are ready to proceed.

When the only copy is a photograph

A photograph of a tablet can be useful as a reminder, but glare, automatic cropping and a folded cover sometimes hide the footer. Before relying on the photograph, look for pagination, a revision identifier and any reference to an appendix. An illustrated diagram showing the intended incision might sit on a separate page. A paragraph about possible blood transfusion may refer to another document. Ask the records desk whether your download includes those items. Keep a brief inventory with a filename and page count for each attachment. This makes it easier to spot an incomplete transfer when switching from a hotel computer to a personal laptop.

If your concern is a discrepancy between languages, retain the exact passage in both versions. Highlight the sentence in a working copy and describe the difference plainly. For example, one version might describe an overnight stay while the other mentions discharge the same evening. Request clarification about the expected arrangement and its practical effects, including transport, accommodation and access to the ward after departure. An ordinary scheduling discrepancy may have a simple explanation. A discrepancy that influenced your choice deserves a dated answer from someone familiar with the clinical plan.
