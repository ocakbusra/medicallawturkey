Insurance After a Medical Complication in Türkiye: What Should Patients Check?

A guide to identifying possible insurance policies, preserving documents and asking about coverage after treatment in Türkiye.

Different policies serve different purposes

A patient may encounter travel insurance, a policy associated with a treatment package, complication cover or a healthcare professional’s liability insurance. The name of a policy does not establish what it covers, who can claim or whether a particular loss is payable.

Collect the policy and schedule

Ask for the insurer’s name, policy number, full wording, schedule, insured parties, coverage period, exclusions, notification requirements and claim contact. Keep package documents, receipts, correspondence and any written explanation of cover. If the clinic refers to insurance, ask which policy it means.

Questions for the insurer

Ask who is entitled to notify or claim, whether treatment abroad or after returning home is included, what documentation is required, and whether prior approval or a deadline applies. Record the response and request written confirmation. Avoid assuming routine travel insurance covers elective treatment or later corrective care.

Coverage is separate from legal responsibility

A policy may address a complication, an alleged liability or another defined risk; these are different questions. Whether any direct claim or recovery is available depends on the policy, applicable law and facts. An insurer’s response does not by itself resolve whether care was negligent.

Before accepting an offer

Check whether a payment or release settles further claims and whether it addresses future treatment. Obtain individual advice before signing. Read what to check in a refund or revision release and possible case costs.

Official sources

Check the current text and obtain advice on how it applies to your circumstances.

  • Turkish Code of Obligations, Law No. 6098

Identify the policy before assessing cover

A reference to insurance in a package advertisement is a starting point, not the policy wording. Ask whether a separate policy was issued, who purchased it and whether you are named or otherwise eligible to benefit. Request the certificate, schedule, conditions and any endorsements. If the provider cannot supply them, ask for the insurer or broker's contact details. Keep the advertisement and sales messages, but distinguish what was said during booking from the insurer's actual written response and the policy terms.

Ask about notification and approvals

Insurance documents may contain conditions about when and how to report an event, who may report it and what information must be supplied. Some treatment expenses may be subject to approval requirements or exclusions. Read the relevant documents and ask the insurer to explain the process in writing; do not assume a clinic notification automatically completes every step. If care is medically urgent, follow appropriate clinical advice and contact the insurer as soon as practical without allowing an administrative question to delay necessary treatment.

Record costs and responses separately

Create a list of medical consultations, tests, prescriptions, travel and other costs, supported by itemised invoices and evidence of payment. Record which sums were paid by you, a public health service or an insurer. If an insurer reimburses an expense, keep the settlement statement and note what it covers. An estimate for future care should be labelled as an estimate. Whether an expense is insured or potentially recoverable in a legal claim requires separate consideration; the same amount should not be assumed payable twice.

Liability insurance and complication cover

A healthcare professional's liability policy and a policy designed to respond to specified complications may have different purposes, insured parties and conditions. A policy's existence does not itself prove negligence, and a refusal under one policy does not decide every legal question. Ask for the policy category and full terms rather than relying on a verbal description. Whether a patient may approach a particular insurer directly depends on the applicable law, policy and circumstances and should be checked with a qualified lawyer.

Questions before a settlement

Ask whether an offer concerns one invoice, an insurance payment or a full and final settlement. Request the complete wording, including any release, waiver, confidentiality or future-treatment terms, and give yourself time to understand it. If the document is in another language, arrange a reliable translation. Save all versions and ask whether a proposed payment addresses treatment that has not yet been assessed. Our guide to revision and refund releases provides further questions to discuss.

When the provider says a policy exists

Request the identity of the insurer and ask who is the policyholder, what type of cover is involved and how to obtain the applicable conditions. Keep a dated record of the request and the response. If the policy is issued to a clinician or facility, it may not operate like a policy purchased by the patient. The insurer or a lawyer can explain what information may be available and which route should be considered. Do not send sensitive records to an unverified contact; confirm the organisation and use its stated secure process before transferring documents.
