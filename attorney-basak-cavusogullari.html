Professional profile

Att. Başak Çavuşoğulları is an attorney at law and a member of the Bursa Bar Association. The practice areas presented by Medical Law Türkiye include medical malpractice, health tourism, litigation and coordination of independent expert review. English and Turkish language support is available.

Medical malpractice and health tourism

Başak’s listed work includes medical malpractice and health tourism law, litigation and compensation claims. An assessment can involve identifying the treatment provider, reviewing the agreed procedure and available records, and considering the evidence relevant to care, causation and loss.

The work also includes coordination of independent expert reviews. The questions put to an expert and the records required depend on the medical issues. A court may arrange its own expert assessment.

Work with international patients

A first discussion can begin with the treatment location, approximate dates and a brief description of the concern. How a case review is approached

The legal and clinical questions should be considered separately. A review can identify gaps in documents and whether specialist medical input may assist. A complication or an initial enquiry does not establish malpractice, a right to compensation or an attorney-client relationship.

Preparing to make contact

An initial discussion from abroad

Patients often contact a Turkish lawyer after returning home, when the people involved, treatment records and follow-up arrangements are difficult to understand. An initial discussion can start with the location, dates and a brief description of the concern. It is helpful to distinguish what was agreed, what treatment was recorded and what happened afterwards. If you are still receiving care, say so and follow the advice of your treating clinician. A legal review is not a substitute for medical assessment.

Reviewing the treatment journey

The information may come from several sources: a booking conversation, a clinic invoice, the hospital file and later care at home. The legal team can identify which documents are available and which questions need further examination. A practical chronology can record consultations, treatment, symptoms and requests for follow-up. Keep original files where possible and explain when a date or detail is uncertain. The identity of a clinic brand, a treating professional and a contracting company should be checked rather than assumed.

Questions for a medical expert

Başak’s listed work includes coordination of independent expert reviews. A review may require the appropriate specialty, a clear set of questions and sufficiently complete records. The issues can include the treatment described in the file, relevant clinical findings and whether further information is needed to address causation. An independent opinion does not automatically decide a dispute, and a court may seek its own expert evidence. The need, scope and cost of any review should be discussed for the individual matter.

Understanding legal representation

Medical malpractice and health tourism disputes can involve different providers and legal routes. The appropriate steps depend on the facts, applicable rules and evidence. Ask what an initial assessment covers, whether additional records or translations are needed and which potential deadlines require urgent attention. If further work is proposed, request an explanation of the scope, fees and anticipated expenses. No outcome can be guaranteed, and an enquiry does not itself create an attorney-client relationship.

Preparing to contact the team

A concise first message can identify the treatment date, city, provider and the main question you would like to discuss. You can also say whether you have the operative record, consent documents, invoices and a later medical report. Avoid sending full medical files, intimate photographs or identity documents through the public enquiry form. The team can discuss a suitable way to share sensitive documents if needed. See the expert review guide and case costs guide.

What to ask before agreeing on further work

You can ask which records are necessary for the first stage, how missing information will be requested and whether medical expertise is likely to be useful. Discuss who will communicate with you, the language of the advice and how progress will be reported. If translations, external experts or formal document procedures may involve separate costs, ask for those to be explained in a written scope. The steps and timing depend on the circumstances; a useful first discussion should clarify the next decision and what remains uncertain rather than promise a result.
