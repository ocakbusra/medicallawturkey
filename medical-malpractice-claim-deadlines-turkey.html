Medical Malpractice Deadlines in Türkiye: What Affects Your Time Limit?

Understand why medical malpractice deadlines vary and what dates and documents a Turkish lawyer needs to assess them.

There is no single safe deadline

The applicable time limit depends on the legal route, provider, relevant dates and case-specific facts. An online countdown cannot establish your deadline. Seek an individual assessment promptly, including when treatment occurred some time ago.

Dates to gather

Write down the consultation, procedure, discharge, first symptoms, diagnosis, later treatment and when you learned who was involved. Keep the dates on documents and messages as well as the dates you received them. A lawyer can assess which events matter for each possible route; symptoms, diagnosis and knowledge may not occur on the same day.

Why the route matters

Contractual, tort, consumer, public-provider and criminal procedures may involve different rules, conditions or time limits. Multiple providers and treatments can make the assessment more complex. The earliest potentially relevant date should be checked rather than waiting for all records to arrive.

Do not assume discussions pause time

A complaint, request for records, negotiation or promise of revision should not be assumed to stop or extend a deadline. The legal effect of a particular step depends on the governing rules and facts. Ask a lawyer to assess timing before relying on correspondence or an offer.

Prepare for a timing assessment

Send a short chronology, provider identities, agreements, notices and a list of available medical records. Tell the lawyer about prior complaints, proceedings or settlement documents. See preparing for a remote review.

Official sources

Check the current text and obtain advice on how it applies to your circumstances.

  • Turkish Code of Obligations, Law No. 6098
  • Code of Civil Procedure, Law No. 6100

Create a timeline with sources

A useful timeline records the date of each appointment and procedure, the first sign of the problem, follow-up communications and any later diagnosis or corrective treatment. Put the source next to each entry: for example, an appointment email, discharge summary or dated photograph. If you are unsure of a date, label it approximate. Keep your own chronology separate from the medical record rather than changing the original. A lawyer can compare these events with the relevant legal rules and identify which dates require closer examination.

Different questions can have different time limits

A claim may be considered under different legal bases, and public and private providers may involve different procedures. A civil claim, an administrative application and a criminal complaint should not be treated as interchangeable. The treatment date, when an injury became apparent and when the provider was identified can raise different questions. The applicable period, its starting point and any exceptions depend on the route and facts. Do not select a deadline from an online example without an individual assessment.

When symptoms appear after you return home

A patient may initially be told that swelling or discomfort is expected and only later receive a diagnosis from another clinician. Keep both the early communications and the later medical findings. Record what was known at each stage without assuming that a later diagnosis automatically changes the legal deadline. If treatment took place in stages, record each operation and follow-up separately. Tell the lawyer about any continuing treatment or information that has not yet been obtained, because the urgency of a review may remain even when the file is incomplete.

Records, negotiations and earlier steps

Save requests for records, replies, refund offers, revision proposals and the full text of any document you have signed. Inform the lawyer about previous complaints, mediation, litigation or communications from insurers, including where they took place and who received them. Do not assume that negotiations or a promise to investigate extend the time available. Equally, do not assume that a document has a particular legal effect simply because it has a formal heading. Its wording, recipients, timing and the applicable rules need review.

Practical questions to ask

Ask which potential routes require urgent consideration, what information is missing and whether any step must be taken while records are being gathered. Confirm who is responsible for monitoring dates and what work has been agreed. If you have already received a deadline or notice, send its date and issuing authority in your initial summary. This guide cannot calculate an individual limitation period, and the absence of a definitive date is not a reason to wait before seeking advice.

If a provider offers further treatment

An offer of a review or revision can be important to your clinical decisions, but it should be considered separately from the timing of a possible claim. Save the proposed appointment, the explanation of what would be done and any conditions attached. Ask a qualified clinician about the medical aspects and a lawyer about the legal wording and relevant deadlines. A request to wait for a future appointment or a promise that the clinic will investigate should not be treated as a confirmed extension. If you have received a form with a signature deadline, include it in your urgent request for advice.
