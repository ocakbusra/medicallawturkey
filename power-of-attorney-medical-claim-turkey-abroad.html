Power of Attorney for a Turkish Medical Claim: A Guide for Patients Abroad

Understand the purpose, possible signing routes and questions to ask before granting a Turkish lawyer authority from abroad.

What a power of attorney can do

An appropriately drafted and formalised power of attorney can authorise a Turkish lawyer to carry out specified legal steps. The wording and powers should be agreed for the intended work. Granting authority does not guarantee a result or remove every possible need for your participation.

Discuss the scope before signing

Ask what the lawyer needs to do, whether any special authority is requested, and how decisions about settlement, withdrawal, collection or other significant steps will be communicated. Read the text with a qualified translator if necessary. Agree the engagement and fees separately in writing.

Signing outside Türkiye

Depending on eligibility and location, a Turkish consulate or a local notary may be relevant. A foreign document may require an apostille or other authentication and Turkish translation. Requirements vary by country, citizenship, document and use; confirm the exact route, identification, appointment and wording with the lawyer and issuing authority before signing.

What to prepare

Ask the lawyer for their identification and bar details and the precise wording needed. Confirm the document’s language, required original or certified copy, delivery arrangements, costs and any expiry or revocation questions. Do not send identity documents through an unsecured enquiry form.

Can everything happen remotely?

An initial review can often begin remotely, and a lawyer may handle authorised procedural steps. A court, examination, evidence requirement or other matter may still require your participation. Ask about the likely stages for your circumstances.

Official sources

Check the current text and obtain advice on how it applies to your circumstances.

  • Turkish Consulate: power-of-attorney information
  • Code of Civil Procedure, Law No. 6100

A document tailored to the intended work

A power of attorney identifies a representative and grants authority for specified acts. Before arranging an appointment, discuss the purpose of the document and obtain wording appropriate to the planned legal work. Ask the lawyer to explain any special powers and whether they are necessary for your matter. A broad form should not be signed simply because it is described as standard. If the text is in Turkish and you do not understand it, arrange an appropriate explanation or translation before signing.

Compare the available signing routes

Depending on your nationality, location and the service offered, a Turkish consulate or a local notary may be an option. The requirements are not identical in every country. A locally issued document may need an apostille where the relevant convention applies, or another form of authentication where it does not. Translation and certification requirements can also differ. Confirm the route with the receiving lawyer and the authority that will issue or certify the document before paying for an appointment.

Prepare for the appointment

Ask what identification, lawyer details, draft wording, photographs, language support and appointment documents are required. Check fees directly with the issuing authority. Do not assume that a relative can sign on your behalf or that an old document gives the powers needed for a new case. If an interpreter is required, ask the authority what qualifications or arrangements it accepts. The Turkish consular portal publishes information about notarial procedures, but a particular mission may have its own practical requirements.

After signing

Ask how the lawyer will obtain an acceptable copy, whether the original must be sent and how any translation or certification will be arranged. Keep a copy of the signed document and a record of its date and the authority that issued it. Discuss how you will give instructions, receive updates and approve important decisions. If your circumstances change, ask about the process and consequences of changing or withdrawing authority. The power of attorney and the engagement or fee agreement serve different purposes; read both.

Remote representation and participation

A lawyer acting under appropriate authority may be able to handle many legal steps while you are abroad. That does not mean every case can be completed without your involvement. Depending on the facts and procedure, further instructions, documents, an examination or other participation may be needed. Ask what is known at the outset and what may only become clear later. Our remote review guide explains how to organise the initial file.

Questions to take to the appointment

Before paying for certification, check whether the document must be prepared in a particular form and who will provide the final text. Ask whether you need an interpreter, how your name should appear across your identification and the document, and what the issuing authority requires to verify your identity. Confirm whether you will receive an original, a certified copy or another means for the lawyer to obtain it. Requirements for one country or consulate are not necessarily the requirements elsewhere. If there is an inconsistency in the details, ask for it to be corrected through the issuing authority rather than altering the signed document yourself.
