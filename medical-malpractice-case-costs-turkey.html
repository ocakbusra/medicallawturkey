What Costs May Arise Before a Medical Malpractice Case in Türkiye?

Understand possible translation, expert, court, document and representation costs in a Turkish medical-malpractice matter without relying on a generic quote. Estimated reading time: 6 minutes

Short answer

There is no reliable one-size-fits-all total for a Turkish medical-malpractice matter. Depending on the route and case, expenses may include legal representation, translation, document certification, court charges, service and expert costs. Ask for a written, case-specific estimate and confirm what is included before authorising work.

Updated: 25 September 2026. This is general legal information, not advice on an individual matter.

Why online totals can mislead

Two matters involving cosmetic treatment can require very different work. One may need only a focused record review; another may involve several providers, records in multiple languages, expert questions, court proceedings or later treatment. The legal route, amount in dispute, number of parties and evidence needed can all affect the work.

A headline figure may leave out translation, expert review, court-related payments, notarial or consular documents, travel or additional procedural steps. It may also describe an attorney fee but not the expenses paid to third parties. Ask what the figure includes, what it excludes and when payment may be due.

This page provides categories to ask about, not a quote, fee schedule or prediction of what will be recoverable. Current official tariffs and the facts of the matter must be checked when advice is given.

Possible cost categories

  • Initial legal assessment: ask whether the fee covers a short consultation, a document review, written advice or a further scope of work.
  • Representation: confirm the work stages included, who will handle the matter, how additional work is authorised and how fees are invoiced.
  • Translation: medical records, agreements, messages or court documents may need translation. The format needed can depend on the purpose and procedure.
  • Document formalities: a power of attorney or foreign public document may involve notarial, consular, certification or delivery costs depending on the country and use.
  • Court-related expenses: applicable filing or procedural charges, service costs and other payments may arise if a case proceeds.
  • Expert work: an independent medical review or a court-directed expert process may involve separate fees or advances.
  • Practical expenses: secure copies, courier, travel or time away from work may be relevant to your own budget.

Not every category applies to every case. Some items may be avoidable, included in a quoted scope or payable at different stages. Confirm the details with the lawyer handling the file.

Ask for a written scope and estimate

Before authorising work, ask the firm to set out:

  • What the initial assessment covers and which documents will be reviewed.
  • What work is included if the matter moves to a formal notice, negotiation or court process.
  • Which expenses are charged separately and whether they need advance payment.
  • How translation, expert opinions, additional parties or an appeal would change the scope.
  • How the fee is calculated, invoiced and updated if the case changes.
  • Whether any costs may be requested from another party and what uncertainty remains.

Do not rely on a verbal estimate if the scope is important to your decision. Ask for a written engagement proposal and read it before signing. A fee agreement should make the service and payment terms understandable.

Budget records and translations efficiently

Start with an inventory rather than translating every file immediately. List each document, language, length, source and why you think it matters. A lawyer can identify which records are needed for an initial assessment and which translations may be required later.

Article 223 of the Code of Civil Procedure addresses foreign-language documents submitted in civil proceedings: the party relying on such a document must submit a translation, and the court may require an official translation. This does not mean that an informal working translation is useless for an early discussion, or that every document needs the same certification. Confirm the requirement before placing an order.

For expert review, ask what specialty and questions are within scope. A broad, unstructured request may cost more and still leave the central medical issue unresolved. See how to prepare a focused expert-review file.

Keep case costs separate from potential compensation

Amounts paid for travel, translation or treatment do not automatically become recoverable damages. Whether an expense is legally relevant, supported and recoverable depends on the facts and applicable rules. Do not count a possible recovery as guaranteed income when deciding whether to proceed.

Likewise, a clinic’s offer to reimburse one expense does not necessarily address other treatment costs or the effect of a settlement document. Review the full offer before accepting it. Our guide to revision and refund releases explains what to check.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a medical-malpractice case cost in Türkiye?

There is no reliable universal total. Costs depend on the case route, work requested, documents, translations, experts and current official charges.

Are court and expert costs always required?

No. They depend on whether and how a matter proceeds. Ask counsel which costs may arise at each stage.

Can I get every legal expense back if I win?

Do not assume that. Recoverability and amount are decided under the applicable rules and facts; a lawyer should explain the risk.

Should I translate all medical records before speaking to a lawyer?

Usually start by making an inventory and asking which records need translation for the first assessment. Court requirements may differ.
